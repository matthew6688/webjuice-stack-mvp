# Design Review · v7-od-warm-editorial (OD `warm-editorial` design system · book-stock cream + Georgia serif)

## 5-Dim Scoresheet (each 0-10)
- Philosophy consistency: **9/10** · DESIGN-NOTES claims "cream canvas + serif display + calm publication rhythm + one accent" and the HTML delivers it without leaks. Tokens pasted verbatim, no second `:root`, zero green (the system explicitly does not carry a forest secondary — V2's moss `#2d3a2a` is absent), zero off-palette colour, Georgia is the only display family, Inter the only body family. The single `--accent: #9b5b32` terracotta is reused in seven coordinated places (H1 accent span, step numbers, review stars, area-note left-border, sticky-call, primary CTAs, link underlines) and nowhere else. The contact section is full-dark `--fg` background with underline-only inputs, exactly as DESIGN.md prescribes. Loses 1pt because the `<span class="accent">last another generation.</span>` italic accent phrase in the H1 lands on a marketing cliché that the otherwise restrained voice doesn't earn.
- Visual hierarchy: **9/10** · The strongest hierarchy of the eight variants. H1 88px Georgia + lead 20px Inter + meta strip + 2 CTAs is a textbook editorial first screen. The process section's "01/02/03/04" oversized serif numerals sitting under a 2px black horizontal rule is genuinely newspaper section-divider grammar — it gives the page a structural beat that v0/v2/v5 don't have. The dark contact section uses inversion as a hierarchical signal: the page goes black-on-cream → cream-on-black at the conversion moment, which is one of the cleanest "now do the thing" gradients in the set. Loses 1pt because the about-section pull-quote with a terracotta vertical rule competes slightly with the process section's serif numerals — both want to be the "structural editorial moment".
- Detail execution: **9/10** · Token-traceable warm `#fbf6ee` cream (creamier than V2's `#f4efe6`), warm-brown foreground `#201914` (vs V2's slate `#1c1f24`), the 2px black rule + serif numeral process step, terracotta vertical rule on the area-note callout, hand-pointed-ridges field-note caption on the hero figure, underline-only inputs on the dark contact section with a `--surface-warm` focus underline. The `Vicwest<em>.</em>` brand wordmark with a Georgia-italic full stop is the kind of detail v0 / v6 never reach for. Mono labels in the dark form (uppercase 12px) instead of sentence-case sit on a real editorial convention.
- Function (conversion path · CTA prominence · trust signals): **6/10** · Phone is in the nav as a `Request a quote` button (not the phone number directly — a small loss vs v0/v6 which keep `0403 554 592` in the nav). Two real CTAs on the hero (Request quote + Call) at 48px min-height. Sticky terracotta pill phone-CTA on mobile. Form is in a dark `--fg` contact section which is high-contrast and unambiguously "this is the conversion moment". Loses 4pts because: (a) 112px section padding produces a long scroll, (b) underline-only inputs on the dark contact section will hurt completion for the 55+ Ballarat homeowner who expects boxed fields (same problem v1 has), (c) the area-section lists only 7 suburbs in the JSON-LD vs 16 in v0/v5/v6 — measurable SEO loss for a service-area business.
- Innovation (departure from AI default): **7/10** · The 2px-black-rule + serif-numeral process section is uncommon and structurally good. The single-warm-axis palette with zero secondary chroma is more disciplined than v2/v5 in the same lane. The dark contact section with underline-only inputs is a real editorial gesture. Loses 3pts because the underlying aesthetic (cream + Georgia + terracotta) is recognisably inside the same 2024-2025 "warm editorial" current consensus as v2/v4/v5 — distinguishable but adjacent. v3 holds more singular moat (Noto Serif JP extralight + 200px gaps is uncopyable; cream + Georgia + terracotta is the Squarespace-template-of-the-year).
- **Total: 40/50**

## Top 3 strengths
- The process section (4 steps under a 2px black rule with `01/02/03/04` in oversized terracotta Georgia) is the single best structural innovation across all eight variants. It introduces a step the customer actually wants to see ("how does this work?") in a register no Ballarat competitor will reach for.
- Accent discipline is the cleanest in the warm-editorial lane: one terracotta `#9b5b32` doing exactly seven coordinated jobs, zero secondary chroma, zero green. V2 has moss + brick; V5 has caramel doing five jobs; V7 has terracotta doing seven jobs each of which is intentional.
- The dark-on-cream → cream-on-dark inversion at the contact section is a real editorial gesture — it tells the reader "the page has finished talking, now you talk". Better than v5's same-paper-throughout contact and better than v6's elevated white form tile.

## Top 3 weaknesses
- Underline-only inputs on the dark contact section will measurably hurt form completion for the 55+ demographic — same Vignelli-trap as v1. Inversion + dark mode + underline-only is three departures from the boxed-cream-input default, and they compound.
- The phone-number-as-text in the nav is replaced by `Request a quote` button — a real loss of one-click-to-call. v0/v6 keep the actual digits visible in the masthead at all times; v7 hides them behind a verb.
- Service-area coverage is narrower in the JSON-LD (7 suburbs) than v0/v5/v6 (16 suburbs). For a roofer whose entire business is "do you work in MY suburb?", this is a measurable SEO and trust loss.

## Top 5 concrete fix recommendations
1. Swap the nav `Request a quote` button text to the actual phone number `0403 554 592` (keep the button style) — one-click-to-call from the nav is non-negotiable for a trade business · selector `.nav-cta`
2. Drop the `last another generation.` accent phrase in the H1; replace with something that earns the italic — e.g. `built right, and signed off in writing.` — or remove the `<span class="accent">` entirely and let the H1 sit plain · selector `#hero-h1 .accent`
3. Lighten the dark-form inputs from underline-only to a 1px-solid `--surface-warm` outline on a 4px `--radius-sm` — preserves the inversion drama without sacrificing the affordance · selectors `.contact .field input`, `.contact .field select`, `.contact .field textarea`
4. Expand JSON-LD `areaServed` from 7 to 16 suburbs to match the visible suburb chip strip — keep the visible list as is (the chips are good), just close the SEO gap · `<script type="application/ld+json">` block
5. Cap section padding at 96px (currently 112px) — the process section + reviews band already give the page enough breathing room; the additional 16px per section pushes mobile scroll-to-CTA past the 3-screen threshold · `:root { --section-y-desktop: 96px; }`

## Audience fit
The 45-65 Ballarat homeowner who reads quotes carefully and trusts a "considered" register over a "modern" one. Soldiers Hill / Lake Wendouree / Buninyong heritage and weatherboard owners. Also works for the architect-referred or insurance-claim job where the customer wants to see process before price. Slightly too restrained for the median Sebastopol quote-shopper but credible across more of the market than v3.

## Verdict
- Recommended ship lane: **YES-with-fixes**
- Must-fix items: (1) phone number back into the nav, (2) drop the H1 accent phrase, (3) lighten dark inputs to outlined fields. Once those land, v7 is the most balanced "warm editorial" variant — better hierarchy than v2/v5, broader audience than v3, more conviction than v4 (no italic-copper-word tic, no floating-pill nav, no double-bezel template signature).
