# Design Review · v6-od-bento (OD `bento` design system · Notion/Linear tile composition)

## 5-Dim Scoresheet (each 0-10)
- Philosophy consistency: **8/10** · DESIGN-NOTES claims a tile composition extended across the whole page — not just services — and the HTML actually delivers it. Nav is a rounded `--radius-lg` tile with `--elev-raised` and backdrop blur (not a flat bar), hero is 4 tiles on a shared 12-col grid (headline 7 + image 5×2 + metrics 7 + area 5), about is photo-tile + copy-tile + 4-fact strip, reviews is 1-large + 2-stacked, gallery pairs are mini-bentos. Token discipline is solid: verbatim `:root` paste, every spacing and radius via `var(--*)`. Loses 2pts because the philosophy is Notion/Linear/Apple-Notes — a SaaS dashboard language — applied to a roofing trade business. The mismatch is real: bento was invented for product surfaces with status pills and stat widgets, and the "Available this week" green-dot status pill on the hero hero tile is the moment the metaphor strains. A Ballarat homeowner does not expect a `/dashboard` from their roofer.
- Visual hierarchy: **8/10** · The asymmetric 7/5 hero grid + tall image tile spanning 2 rows is genuinely a more interesting first impression than v0's banner. The 1-feature + 1-side + 4-half services bento gives Replacement (the highest-margin trade) deserved visual weight. Section heads use a 2-col layout (eyebrow+H2 left, ledger note right) that scans well. Loses 2pts because at desktop ≥1280px the 24px radius + soft borders + 16px gaps make every tile feel like the same tile — the relative weight between hero, metrics, and area chips is closer than it should be. Linear gets away with this because it's a product list; a roofing site needs the H1 to dominate.
- Detail execution: **7/10** · Clean and consistent: 36px brand-mark square with `VW` mono monogram, `01 · Featured` mono tag on the lead service tile, "Available this week" status dot in `--success` green, sticky bottom-CTA as a floating tile with the same border/radius/blur as the nav (the metaphor pays its dues on mobile). Loses 3pts because the details don't accumulate the way v3/v4 do: there is no equivalent of the drop-cap, the Plate caption, the cubic-bezier vocabulary. The radial blue gradient behind the hero (`rgba(37,99,235,0.16)`) is the strongest detail moment and it is *also* the moment the page reads "Stripe landing, 2023" most loudly.
- Function (conversion path · CTA prominence · trust signals): **8/10** · This is v6's best dimension. Phone number is a coloured `--btn-primary` always visible in the nav. Hero shows two real CTAs + a `Available this week` status pill + 3 metric tiles in the second hero row. Sticky 2-col mobile CTA (Call + Quote) is the most generous mobile conversion strip in the set. Form sits in section VII inside a `--elev-raised` tile that signals "this is the important thing". Loses 2pts for `Request a free quote` (free is the AI-default verb — `Request a written quote` is more credible) and for the 18-suburb chip strip being truncated to 5 with `+11 more` on the hero — a measurable SEO loss vs v0/v5 which list all 16.
- Innovation (departure from AI default): **5/10** · This is the dimension where v6 is most exposed. Bento-on-everything reads as Notion or Linear or Apple Notes in 2025, which means it reads as AI-template default. Blue accent `#2563eb` is the literal Tailwind blue. Inter + SF Mono is the literal AI-default font pairing. 24px-radius tiles with soft borders is the most photocopied web aesthetic of the last two years. The execution is competent but a competitor with a Framer template subscription ships this monthly. None of the bento moves are *wrong*; collectively they constitute the median of high-quality AI output, not a departure from it.
- **Total: 36/50**

## Top 3 strengths
- The 4-tile hero (headline + tall image + metrics + area chips) is a structurally more honest first screen than the standard hero banner — it shows the customer four different proof-vectors at once instead of asking them to scroll.
- Conversion infrastructure is the best of the seven variants: always-visible phone CTA in the nav tile, sticky 2-col mobile bottom bar, `Available this week` micro-availability signal, elevated form tile in section VII. v6 will measurably out-convert v1/v3/v5/v7 on the median Sebastopol/Wendouree quote-shopper.
- The token contract is real and clean — Inter + SF Mono + JetBrains Mono + a tight type scale + 24/16/10 radius ladder all resolve via `var(--*)`. A junior dev extending this page will not break the system by accident.

## Top 3 weaknesses
- The bento aesthetic is the dominant 2024-2025 AI default. Blue accent + Inter + rounded tiles + soft borders + radial gradient hero glow is what every AI agent ships when asked for "modern SaaS landing". There is zero competitive moat — a Ballarat competitor copies this in a weekend.
- The visual register is wrong for the customer. Roofing is a 20-year-warranty trade that wants to read "trustworthy, established, written-quote". Bento reads "product launch, status pill, available-now". The "Available this week" green dot in the hero is the clearest single tell that the language doesn't fit the brand.
- The page sacrifices 16-suburb SEO surface area (truncated to 5 + `+11 more` in the hero area tile) to keep the hero composition tidy — the full 16-list area section later in the page partly recovers this but the hero chip is a missed signal.

## Top 5 concrete fix recommendations
1. Drop the `Available this week` green status pill — it imports a SaaS-dashboard convention into a trade context where "available" is permanent (Mon-Fri 7-5 is in the hours block) · selector `.status` on the hero headline tile
2. Swap the radial blue hero gradient for a token-driven `--surface-warm` wash — keeps the bento atmosphere but removes the 2023-Stripe fingerprint · `.page { background: ... }`
3. Change `Request a free quote` → `Request a written quote` everywhere (3 button instances) — "free" reads as marketing default; "written" reads as a credible trade promise · text in `.btn-primary` instances
4. Expand the hero area-tile chips from 5+`+11 more` to all 8 primary suburbs (Ballarat Central, Sebastopol, Wendouree, Buninyong, Delacombe, Mount Pleasant, Lake Wendouree, Soldiers Hill) — the SEO + trust value of seeing your suburb on the hero outweighs the layout tidiness · selector `.hero-area .area-list`
5. Reduce tile radius from 24px to 16px for the secondary tiles (metrics, area, about-facts) and keep 24px only on hero/nav/sticky-CTA so radius does hierarchical work — currently every tile is the same shape · selectors `.metric`, `.fact`, `.hero-area`

## Audience fit
The 30-45 year-old design-aware homeowner who works in tech / product / professional services and is fluent in SaaS UI. Lives in Alfredton or Mount Pleasant, renovating a 2010s-2020s build, expects software-like product interfaces from every brand. Wrong register for the 55+ Soldiers Hill / Buninyong heritage owner and wrong for the median Sebastopol / Wendouree quote-comparison shopper.

## Verdict
- Recommended ship lane: **YES-with-fixes, but only as a niche option, NOT as a default**
- Must-fix items: (1) drop the status pill, (2) replace the radial blue gradient with a warm token wash, (3) `free` → `written` across all CTAs. Even after fixes, v6 is the most-replicable variant in the library — its conversion machinery is strong but its visual differentiation is the weakest. Ship for tech-buyer clients only; don't make it the default.
